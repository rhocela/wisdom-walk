## Historical Context

Genesis 1 was written in a world filled with **competing creation myths**—Babylonian *Enuma Elish*, Egyptian accounts, Canaanite stories. These pagan narratives depicted chaotic battles between gods, creation from violence, and humanity as an afterthought (slaves to the gods).

Against this backdrop, Genesis 1 is revolutionary: **One God**, creating peacefully by His word. No conflict, no rivals. Humanity is not a mistake or afterthought but the climax of creation, made **in God's own image**. The sun and moon—worshiped as deities by surrounding nations—are merely "lights" God creates to serve humanity.

## Connecting the Dots

Genesis 1 opens with "In the beginning, God created"—and *John 1:1-5* deliberately echoes this, revealing that "In the beginning was **the Word**." What Moses presents as God's powerful speech creating everything ("And God said..."), John identifies as the pre-incarnate Christ. The Word wasn't just God's tool; the Word *was* God, and through Him all things were made. Every "Let there be" in Genesis is Christ speaking creation into existence.

*Colossians 1:15-17* expands this further: Christ is **"the image of the invisible God"** and "by him all things were created... all things were created through him and for him." The God who made humanity "in our image" in Genesis is revealed to be Christ Himself—the perfect image. Creation wasn't a past event that's done; Christ actively holds it together ("in him all things hold together").

The seventh day of creation ends with God resting from His work—but this rest wasn't because God was tired. It marked completion, satisfaction, wholeness. This same creative power now operates in redemption. When someone trusts in Christ, they become **"a new creation"** (*2 Corinthians 5:17*)—the old passes away, the new comes. The God who spoke "Let there be light" into darkness now speaks spiritual life into dead hearts.

And where is all this heading? *Revelation 21:1-5* shows the final destination: **"new heavens and a new earth."** The same God who created in Genesis will create again—not from nothing this time, but from a fallen world. "Behold, I am making all things new" echoes those original creative commands. From Genesis creation to Revelation new creation, Christ is the beginning and the end, the Alpha and Omega—the same creative Word bringing everything to completion.

## Gospel Connection

The gospel is God's new creation project. Just as God spoke light into primordial darkness in Genesis 1, He speaks spiritual life into dead hearts (2 Corinthians 4:6): "For God, who said, 'Let light shine out of darkness,' has shone in our hearts to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ."

When someone comes to faith in Christ, they become **"a new creation"** (2 Corinthians 5:17)—the old has passed away, the new has come. This isn't mere improvement or renovation; it's the same creative power that brought the universe into existence now applied to spiritually dead souls. The God who said "Let there be light" now says "Let there be life," and dead sinners are raised to walk in newness of life.

## Reflect

1. **What does it mean that you bear God's image?** How should recognizing the image of God in yourself and others change the way you interact with people today—especially those you find difficult?

2. **Genesis 1 shows God creating with effortless power, yet Revelation 21 shows Him coming to dwell with His people.** Why would the all-powerful Creator desire intimate relationship with His creation? What does this reveal about God's heart?
