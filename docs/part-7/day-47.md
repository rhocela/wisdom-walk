# Day 47: The God Who Calls

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 30-32 minutes*

<ProgressTracker dayNumber={47} />

## Chronological Setting

We continue in the **Egyptian Bondage & Exodus** period (c. 1500-1400 BC) as God begins His direct intervention to fulfill His covenant promises to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. After forty years in Midian, Moses encounters the living God at Mount Horeb (Sinai), where he receives his divine commission to lead Israel out of Egypt. This burning bush encounter marks the beginning of the greatest deliverance in Old Testament history and establishes patterns of divine calling that extend throughout Scripture.

## Historical Context

Mount Horeb, also known as Sinai, becomes the sacred mountain where God reveals Himself to Moses and later gives the Law to Israel. Moses' encounter with the burning bush occurs during his routine shepherding duties, demonstrating that God often calls people in the midst of ordinary circumstances. The divine name "I AM WHO I AM" (YHWH) reveals God's eternal, self-existent nature and becomes the covenant name by which Israel will know their Deliverer.

The signs God provides—the staff becoming a serpent, the leprous hand, and water turning to blood—serve both to convince Moses of the reality of his calling and to authenticate his message before both the Israelites and Pharaoh. These miraculous signs demonstrate God's power over creation, disease, and the forces that Egyptians considered divine.

## Redemptive Significance

Moses' calling establishes the **pattern of divine election and commissioning** that runs throughout salvation history. God chooses unlikely candidates—a stammering fugitive shepherd—to accomplish His greatest works, demonstrating that deliverance comes from divine power, not human qualifications. This principle finds its ultimate expression in God choosing weak, foolish, and lowly people to shame the wise and strong (1 Corinthians 1:27-28).

The **burning bush that is not consumed** serves as a powerful symbol of God's presence that preserves rather than destroys. This prefigures how God's holiness, rather than consuming sinners, becomes the means of their preservation and sanctification through Christ. The holy ground where Moses removes his sandals points forward to the access believers have to God's presence through Christ's mediation.

## Connecting the Dots

**The divine "I AM"** revealed to Moses finds its ultimate fulfillment in Jesus' repeated "I am" statements in John's Gospel. When Christ declares "Before Abraham was, I am" (John 8:58), He identifies Himself with the YHWH who appeared to Moses, claiming the same eternal, self-existent nature that defines deity.

**The pattern of divine calling** connects Moses to later prophets like Isaiah, who also encountered God's holiness and received commission despite feeling inadequate. Paul's dramatic encounter with the risen Christ on the Damascus road parallels Moses' burning bush experience—both involve divine light, divine voice, and life-transforming commission from the God who calls the unlikely.

**God's use of ordinary objects** (Moses' staff) to accomplish extraordinary purposes foreshadows how Christ uses simple bread and wine to convey profound spiritual realities, and how the cross—an instrument of shame—becomes the means of salvation. Divine power transforms the mundane into the miraculous.

## Theological Threads

- **God's Faithfulness to His Covenant**: The calling of Moses demonstrates that God remembers His promises even when centuries pass. His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob remains active and will be fulfilled despite human circumstances or delays.

- **Divine Holiness and Human Inadequacy**: The burning bush encounter reveals both God's transcendent holiness that demands reverence (holy ground, removed sandals) and His gracious condescension that enables relationship with sinful humans. This tension requires divine mediation.

- **The Sovereignty of Calling**: God's choice of Moses despite his protests reveals that divine calling depends on God's will rather than human qualifications or willingness. God equips whom He calls, providing both authority and ability for the tasks He assigns.

- **The God Who Sees and Acts**: The divine name "I AM" emphasizes God's active presence in history. He is not a distant deity but one who sees His people's affliction, hears their cries, and acts decisively to deliver them according to His covenant promises.

## Study Questions

1. **Holy Ground**: What does Moses' need to remove his sandals teach us about approaching God? How does Christ's mediation change our access to God's presence while maintaining reverence for His holiness?

2. **Divine Names**: How does God's self-revelation as "I AM WHO I AM" comfort and challenge you? What does this eternal, unchanging name mean for your understanding of God's character and promises?

3. **Reluctant Service**: Moses offered multiple excuses to avoid God's calling (Exodus 4:1, 10, 13). What excuses do people commonly offer to avoid God's purposes for their lives? How does God's response to Moses encourage reluctant servants?

4. **Ordinary Transformed**: How did God use Moses' simple shepherd's staff for miraculous purposes? What ordinary aspects of your life might God want to use for His extraordinary purposes?

5. **Unlikely Calling**: Why does God consistently choose unlikely people for significant roles in His redemptive plan? How does this pattern give hope to those who feel inadequate for God's calling on their lives?
