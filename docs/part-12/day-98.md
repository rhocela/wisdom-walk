# Day 98: Samson's Divine Calling

*Study Samson's miraculous birth announcement and the beginning of God's plan to deliver Israel through an unlikely hero with extraordinary strength and tragic weaknesses.*

## Chronological Setting

**Time Period**: ~1100 BC during the period of the Judges  
**Key Figures**: The Angel of the LORD, Manoah, Manoah's wife, young Samson  
**Location**: Zorah in the tribal territory of Dan  
**Duration**: Approximately 2-3 years from Samson's birth announcement to his young adulthood

This study covers the transition from the minor judges to the introduction of Samson, Israel's most famous and complex judge, whose story reveals both God's faithfulness and the dangers of spiritual compromise.

## Historical Context

### The Minor Judges (Judges 12)
After Jephthah's death, Israel was led by three minor judges whose brief reigns provided stability but little spiritual renewal. Ibzan, Elon, and Abdon each judged Israel for periods ranging from seven to ten years, representing a time of relative peace but also spiritual decline.

### The Philistine Oppression
During this period, the Philistines emerged as Israel's most formidable enemy. Unlike previous oppressors who attacked from outside Canaan, the Philistines had established themselves along the coastal plain and were technologically superior, possessing iron weapons and advanced military tactics.

### God's Preparation for Deliverance
Despite Israel's continued unfaithfulness, God was preparing a deliverer whose very conception would be miraculous. The announcement to Manoah and his wife parallels other divine birth announcements throughout Scripture, demonstrating God's sovereign choice in raising up leaders for His people.

## Redemptive Significance

### Divine Initiative in Barrenness
Like Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, and Hannah, Manoah's wife was barren until God intervened. This pattern throughout Scripture emphasizes that God's greatest works often come through human impossibility, highlighting His sovereign power and grace.

### The Nazirite Calling
Samson was set apart as a Nazirite from birth, dedicated to God with specific restrictions regarding wine, hair cutting, and contact with dead bodies. This calling represented total consecration to the LORD and foreshadowed the ultimate separation and dedication found in Christ.

### Unlikely Instruments of Deliverance
God's choice of Samson—a man who would struggle with moral compromise throughout his life—demonstrates that God can use flawed individuals to accomplish His purposes while also warning of the consequences of unfaithfulness to our divine calling.

## Connecting the Dots

### Birth Announcements and Divine Purpose
The angel's announcement to Manoah's wife parallels the announcements to Mary about Jesus and to Zechariah about John the Baptist. Each announcement reveals God's initiative in salvation history, His use of ordinary people for extraordinary purposes, and His faithfulness to His promises of deliverance.

### Strength and Weakness
Samson's story foreshadows the New Testament teaching that God's strength is perfected in human weakness. While Samson possessed supernatural physical strength, his moral and spiritual weaknesses would ultimately lead to both his downfall and his greatest victory, pointing forward to Christ's victory through apparent defeat.

### The Pattern of Divine Calling
From Samson's calling to Paul's conversion, Scripture reveals that God often chooses unlikely individuals—the barren, the weak, the foolish by worldly standards—to demonstrate that salvation and victory come from Him alone, not from human strength or wisdom.

## Theological Threads

### **Sovereign Grace in Calling**: God's choice of Samson before his birth demonstrates divine election and grace, showing that our calling and purpose come from God's initiative, not our merit or qualifications.

### **Consecration and Compromise**: Samson's Nazirite vow represents the call to holiness that every believer receives, while his struggles with compromise warn us about the danger of compromising our dedication to God.

### **Divine Strength vs. Human Weakness**: The contrast between Samson's physical strength and moral weakness points to the truth that apart from God's Spirit, even the strongest individuals are spiritually vulnerable and need divine grace for faithful living.
