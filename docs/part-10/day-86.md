# Day 86: Be Strong and Courageous

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 25-27 minutes*

<ProgressTracker dayNumber={86} />

## Chronological Setting

Moses is dead; the mourning period is complete. Joshua, son of Nun and faithful servant of Moses for forty years, now faces the enormous responsibility of leading Israel into the Promised Land. God appears to him with direct commission, repeated encouragement, and specific promises. The conquest of Canaan—delayed for forty years due to the previous generation's unbelief—is about to begin. Israel stands at the Jordan River, ready to cross into their inheritance under new leadership.

## Historical Context

**God's direct commission** (Joshua 1:1-9) begins a new era in Israel's history. The phrase "after the death of Moses the servant of the LORD" marks the transition from law-giving to conquest, from preparation to possession. God addresses Joshua directly—not through Moses as intermediary—confirming his new role as Israel's leader. The commission includes territorial promises ("every place that the sole of your foot will tread"), conditional promises (success dependent on obedience to the law), and personal promises (divine presence and protection).

**The repetition of "be strong and courageous"** appears three times in God's commission (Joshua 1:6, 7, 9), emphasizing the courage required for the task ahead. Conquering Canaan would require military courage against formidable enemies, moral courage to obey God's law completely, and spiritual courage to trust God's promises despite overwhelming odds.

**The law as foundation** (Joshua 1:7-8) establishes that successful conquest depends on obedience to Moses' teachings. Joshua must meditate on the law day and night, not turning from it "to the right hand or to the left." Military strategy and divine power must be guided by revealed truth. This principle establishes theocratic leadership—rule according to God's revealed will rather than human wisdom alone.

**The people's response** (Joshua 1:16-18) shows remarkable unity and submission to the new leadership. They pledge absolute obedience to Joshua "just as we obeyed Moses" and promise to execute anyone who rebels against his authority. They even encourage Joshua with the same words God spoke: "Only be strong and courageous!"

**The eastern tribes' commitment** (Joshua 1:12-15) fulfills their promise to Moses. Though Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh had already received their inheritance east of Jordan, their warriors would cross over to help conquer western Canaan. They couldn't settle peacefully while their brothers still faced battles.

## Redemptive Significance

**Joshua as type of Christ** appears clearly in this transition. The name "Joshua" means "Yahweh saves"—the Hebrew equivalent of "Jesus." Just as Joshua succeeded Moses and led Israel into their inheritance, Jesus succeeded the law and leads believers into spiritual rest. Moses (law) brought them to the border; Joshua (grace) brought them into possession.

**The promised rest** prefigures the believer's inheritance in Christ. Hebrews 4:8-9 makes this connection: "For if Joshua had given them rest, God would not have spoken of another day later on. So then, there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God." The physical land points to spiritual rest found in Christ and ultimate rest in the new heaven and earth.

**Divine presence as the key to victory** demonstrates that God's people succeed through His presence, not their own strength. Joshua's confidence came from God's promise: "I will be with you. I will not leave you or forsake you." This promise extends to all believers through Christ: "I will never leave you nor forsake you" (Hebrews 13:5).

**Meditation on God's Word** as the foundation for successful living establishes a principle for all God's people. Joshua's success depended not on military genius or political cunning but on constant attention to God's revealed will. This points forward to Jesus' dependence on Scripture and the Christian's need for regular Bible study and meditation.

## Practical Applications

**1. God's calling comes with God's enabling.** Joshua received an impossible assignment—conquering a land filled with giants and fortified cities with a people recently emerged from nomadic wilderness life. But God's call always includes His empowerment. When God calls us to challenging tasks, He provides the resources needed for success.

**2. Leadership transitions require both divine calling and human support.** Joshua had God's commission but also needed the people's cooperation. Effective leadership involves both vertical accountability to God and horizontal relationships with those being led. Neither divine calling nor human approval alone is sufficient.

**3. Success requires consistent attention to God's Word.** Joshua was commanded to meditate on the law day and night, letting it guide all decisions. Similarly, Christians need regular Bible study not just for spiritual growth but for practical wisdom in daily decisions and challenges.

**4. Courage must be continuously renewed.** God repeated "be strong and courageous" three times, and the people echoed it back to Joshua. Courage isn't a one-time decision but a daily choice to trust God despite circumstances. We need regular reminders of God's presence and promises.

## Key Theological Themes

**Divine sovereignty and human responsibility**: God promised to give Israel the land but required them to actively possess it through obedience and conquest. This balance appears throughout Scripture—God's sovereign grace working through human faithful action.

**The centrality of Scripture**: Success in conquest depended on adherence to Moses' law. This establishes the principle that God's written revelation must guide all aspects of life and leadership. Human wisdom, however impressive, cannot substitute for obedience to God's revealed will.

**The importance of encouragement**: God, Moses, and the people all encouraged Joshua with similar words. Leadership involves both giving and receiving encouragement. Even divinely called leaders need human support and reminder of God's promises.
