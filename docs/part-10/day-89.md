## Chronological Setting

Fresh from the miraculous victory at Jericho, Israel stands poised to continue the conquest of Canaan. The walls fell, the city was destroyed, and God's power was unmistakably displayed. Confidence is high as Joshua sends scouts to assess the next target: Ai, a small city in the hill country. But when Israel attacks Ai, they are soundly defeated, losing thirty-six men and fleeing in panic. The defeat shatters Israel's confidence and strikes terror into Joshua's heart. Unknown to the nation, one man—Achan—has violated God's command by taking devoted things from Jericho for himself. His secret sin has brought corporate guilt upon the entire nation, and God will not give them victory until the sin is dealt with decisively.

## Historical Context

**The Sin at Jericho (Joshua 7:1)**: God had commanded that Jericho be placed under the "ban" (Hebrew: *herem*)—complete consecration to the LORD through destruction. Everything was to be destroyed except the precious metals, which would go into the LORD's treasury, and Rahab's family. Achan, from the tribe of Judah, saw "a beautiful cloak from Shinar, 200 shekels of silver, and a bar of gold weighing 50 shekels" and coveted them. He took them and buried them beneath his tent, directly violating God's explicit command.

**The Defeat at Ai (Joshua 7:2-5)**: Ai was a small city compared to Jericho. The scouts' report seemed reasonable: "Only about 3,000 men need to go up and attack Ai." But when Israel attacked, the men of Ai routed them, killing thirty-six and pursuing them from the city gate. "The hearts of the people melted and became as water"—the same phrase previously used to describe the fear of the Canaanites. Now Israel experiences the terror their enemies had felt.

**God's Revelation and Justice (Joshua 7:10-26)**: Joshua tears his clothes, falls before the ark, and cries out in anguish. God's response is sharp: "Get up! Why have you fallen on your face? Israel has sinned... they have taken some of the devoted things." Through a process of casting lots—first by tribe, then clan, then household, then individual—Achan is identified. He confesses his sin, and he and his entire household are stoned and burned in the Valley of Achor. Only then can Israel proceed with the conquest.

**Victory at Ai (Joshua 8)**: After Achan's sin is judged, God tells Joshua, "Do not fear... Take all the fighting men with you, and arise, go up to Ai. See, I have given into your hand the king of Ai." Using a sophisticated ambush strategy, Israel defeats Ai decisively. The king is hanged on a tree until evening, then buried under a heap of stones. Following this victory, Joshua builds an altar on Mount Ebal and reads the entire law—blessings and curses—before the assembled nation.

## Connecting the Dots

**Corporate Responsibility**: Achan's individual sin brought judgment on the entire nation. This reveals the interconnectedness of God's people—one person's disobedience affects the whole community. Paul warns, "A little leaven leavens the whole lump" (1 Corinthians 5:6).

**The Hiddenness of Sin**: Achan's sin was secret, buried beneath his tent where no one could see. But God saw, and the hidden sin became public judgment. There are no truly private sins—they inevitably affect others and grieve the Holy Spirit.

**Justice Before Mercy**: God's love is not sentimentality that overlooks sin. His holiness demands justice, and sin must be dealt with before blessing can return. The cross demonstrates this perfectly—God's justice satisfied so His mercy can flow freely.

## Theological Threads

- **The Wages of Sin**: Romans 6:23 declares, "The wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." Achan's story illustrates sin's deadly consequences while pointing to our need for Christ's substitutionary death.

- **Hidden Sin Exposed**: Hebrews 4:13 warns, "No creature is hidden from his sight, but all are naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must give account." What we hide from others is fully visible to God.

- **Cleansing the Camp**: Paul's instructions about church discipline (1 Corinthians 5) echo the Achan account—sin must be removed from the community to protect its spiritual health and testimony.

## Study Questions

1. **Hidden Sin**: Achan's sin was secret but affected the entire nation. What "hidden sins" might be undermining your spiritual effectiveness or your family's well-being? How do you bring these into the light for healing?

2. **Corporate Impact**: One man's disobedience caused thirty-six deaths and national defeat. How does understanding the corporate nature of sin affect your personal choices? What responsibility do you bear for your community's spiritual health?

3. **God's Investigation**: God used casting lots to identify Achan's sin, moving methodically from tribe to clan to family to individual. How does God typically expose hidden sin in your life? What role does His Word and Spirit play in conviction?

4. **The Valley of Achor**: Achan was executed in the Valley of Achor (which means "trouble"). Later, God promises to make the Valley of Achor "a door of hope" (Hosea 2:15). How does God transform places of judgment into places of hope? What valleys of trouble have become doors of hope in your life?

5. **Fear After Defeat**: After losing at Ai, Joshua was terrified that Israel would be destroyed and God's name dishonored. When have setbacks or defeats caused you to question God's plan? How do you respond to apparent failure in ministry or life?

6. **Complete Obedience**: God demanded complete destruction at Jericho with no exceptions. Why is partial obedience still disobedience? What areas of your life require complete surrender rather than partial compliance?

7. **Victory After Judgment**: Only after dealing with Achan's sin could Israel defeat Ai. What victories might God be withholding until you deal with specific sins? How do you know when something is hindering your spiritual progress?
