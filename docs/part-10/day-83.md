# Day 83: The Walls Come Down

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 30-33 minutes*

<ProgressTracker dayNumber={83} />

## Chronological Setting

Israel has crossed the Jordan River and camped at Gilgal, just inside the Promised Land. Before beginning the conquest, Joshua leads the nation in two acts of covenant renewal: the circumcision of all males born during the wilderness wandering (reversing the suspension of this covenant sign during the forty years of judgment), and the celebration of Passover in Canaan—their first since leaving Egypt. Immediately after Passover, the manna ceases, and Israel begins eating the produce of the land. Then comes the first and most iconic conquest: the city of Jericho. This fortified city stands as the gateway to Canaan, and God commands an unusual battle strategy—march around the walls for seven days, then shout when the priests blow the trumpets. The walls collapse, Israel conquers the city, and the conquest of the Promised Land begins in earnest.

## Historical Context

**Covenant Renewal (Joshua 5:1-12)**: Before engaging in battle, Israel must be ritually and spiritually prepared. The generation born in the wilderness had not been circumcised—the covenant sign instituted with Abraham (Genesis 17). Joshua circumcises this entire generation at "Gibeath-haaraloth" (Hill of Foreskins), fulfilling God's command and removing "the reproach of Egypt." Then they celebrate Passover—the feast commemorating their deliverance from Egypt—for the first time in the Promised Land. The very next day, the manna ceases after forty years of daily provision. From this point forward, Israel will eat the produce of Canaan.

**The Conquest of Jericho (Joshua 6)**: Jericho was a fortified city with massive walls, strategically positioned to control access to the interior of Canaan. Human military strategy would demand siege engines, battering rams, or starvation tactics. But God's strategy is different: march around the city once a day for six days in complete silence, carrying the ark of the covenant. On the seventh day, march around seven times, then the priests blow the ram's horns (shophar), the people shout, and the walls collapse. Israel rushes in and devotes the city to destruction (herem)—everything destroyed except Rahab and her family, who are spared because she hid the Israelite spies (Joshua 2). The silver, gold, bronze, and iron are consecrated to the LORD's treasury.

This battle establishes the pattern: **victory comes by faith and obedience to God's word, not by human strength**. The ark's presence symbolizes that the LORD Himself fights for Israel.

## Redemptive Significance

Jericho's fall is not merely ancient military history—it's a **picture of how God conquers in every age**. The heavily fortified walls represent everything that stands between humanity and God's purposes: sin's power, Satan's dominion, death's grip, human pride, systemic evil. These are unconquerable by human effort, but they collapse before God's power when His people trust and obey.

**The Ark of the Covenant** led the march around Jericho, symbolizing God's presence and the centrality of His covenant promises. In the same way, Christ—the true presence of God—goes before us in every spiritual battle. We don't conquer through our strength but through His finished work.

**The Seventh Day**: Jericho fell on the seventh day after seven circuits, pointing to God's perfect timing and complete work. The number seven in Scripture signifies completion and perfection. Just as God rested on the seventh day of creation, and Jericho fell on the seventh day, so Christ brings the ultimate victory and rest through His completed work on the cross and His resurrection.

**Rahab's Salvation**: The inclusion of Rahab—a Canaanite prostitute—in Israel's covenant community demonstrates that salvation is by grace through faith, available to anyone who trusts God. Rahab appears in Jesus' genealogy (Matthew 1:5) and is honored in Hebrews 11 and James 2 as a model of saving faith. Her story foreshadows the gospel going to the Gentiles.

## Connecting the Dots

**Faith Conquers the Impossible**: Hebrews 11:30 declares, "By faith the walls of Jericho fell down after they had been encircled for seven days." It wasn't battering rams or military genius—it was faith acting in obedience to God's word. This principle governs all spiritual victory: we walk by faith, not by sight (2 Corinthians 5:7).

**Faith and Works United**: James 2:20-26 uses Rahab as proof that genuine faith produces works. "Faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead" (James 2:17). Rahab's faith was demonstrated by hiding the spies and hanging the scarlet cord from her window. Similarly, Israel's faith was demonstrated by marching around Jericho as commanded, despite how foolish it must have seemed.

**The Scarlet Cord**: Rahab's scarlet cord (Joshua 2:18-21) hanging from her window—the sign that saved her household when Jericho fell—beautifully prefigures Christ's blood. Just as the scarlet cord marked Rahab's house for salvation, so Christ's blood marks believers for deliverance from judgment. The Passover lamb's blood on doorposts in Egypt, the scarlet cord in Jericho, and Christ's blood on the cross all point to the same reality: salvation through blood atonement.

**From Law to Grace**: Israel's journey from Egypt through the wilderness and into Canaan mirrors the believer's journey from slavery to sin, through sanctification, into the rest and inheritance found in Christ. Moses led them to the edge; Joshua brought them across; Jesus brings us all the way into eternal fellowship with God.

## Theological Threads

- **God Fights for His People**: Jericho demonstrates that the battle belongs to the LORD. "The LORD your God is he who goes with you to fight for you against your enemies, to give you the victory" (Deuteronomy 20:4). Our role is faith and obedience; God's role is victory.

- **Spiritual Warfare Principles**: Paul writes, "For the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh but have divine power to destroy strongholds" (2 Corinthians 10:4). Like Jericho's walls, spiritual strongholds don't fall to human wisdom or effort but to God's power released through faith, prayer, and obedience to His word.

- **The Foolishness of God's Methods**: Marching around a walled city seems absurd by human standards, just as the cross seemed foolish to the world. But "the foolishness of God is wiser than men, and the weakness of God is stronger than men" (1 Corinthians 1:25). God often chooses methods that confound human wisdom to ensure He alone receives glory.

- **Corporate Faith and Unity**: The entire nation marched together in unified obedience for seven days. True spiritual victory requires the body of Christ functioning together in faith, not isolated individualism. "How could one have chased a thousand, and two put ten thousand to flight, unless their Rock had sold them?" (Deuteronomy 32:30).

- **Complete Consecration**: Jericho was placed under herem—complete devotion to destruction. Everything was either destroyed or dedicated to the LORD's treasury. This pictures the radical consecration required in spiritual warfare: no compromise with sin, no keeping back what belongs to God (as Achan tragically demonstrated in Joshua 7), total surrender of the "spoils" of victory to God's glory.

- **Grace in the Midst of Judgment**: Even in the wholesale destruction of Jericho, God's grace shines through in Rahab's deliverance. This foreshadows the gospel: God's righteous judgment on sin is real and complete, yet His grace saves all who trust Him, regardless of background or past.

## Study Questions

1. **Covenant Renewal First**: Why did Joshua require circumcision and Passover celebration before the conquest began (Joshua 5:2-10)? What does this teach about the relationship between spiritual preparation and spiritual victory? How do you prepare spiritually before facing life's battles?

2. **The Manna Ceases**: The manna stopped the day after they ate the produce of Canaan (Joshua 5:12). What does this transition teach about God's provision and the different seasons of our spiritual journey? When has God changed His mode of provision in your life, and how did you respond?

3. **Foolish Battle Plans**: If you had been an Israelite soldier, how would you have felt about God's strategy for conquering Jericho—marching silently around walls for seven days? When has God asked you to obey a command that seemed foolish or ineffective by worldly standards? What was the result?

4. **By Faith the Walls Fell**: Hebrews 11:30 attributes Jericho's fall to Israel's faith, not their marching. What "Jericho walls" in your life seem impregnable to human effort but might collapse through persistent, obedient faith? What is God calling you to do while you wait for the breakthrough?

5. **Rahab's Faith and Works**: James 2:25 says Rahab was "justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out by another way." How does Rahab's story help you understand the relationship between faith and works? What "works" flow from your faith in Christ?

6. **The Scarlet Cord**: The scarlet cord saved Rahab's household from destruction (Joshua 2:18-21; 6:22-25). How does this symbol point to Christ's blood? How does understanding salvation as protection under Christ's blood affect your daily assurance and worship?

7. **Complete Victory**: Jericho was completely destroyed—walls collapsed, city burned, inhabitants killed, wealth consecrated to God (Joshua 6:21-24). How does this total conquest picture the thoroughness of Christ's victory over sin, death, and Satan? What "partial victories" or compromises might you be tolerating that God wants to destroy completely?

8. **Journey's End**: We've now completed 83 days tracing redemptive history from creation through Egypt, Sinai, wilderness wandering, and into the Promised Land. How has seeing Christ throughout the Old Testament strengthened your faith? What has been the most surprising or impactful discovery in this journey?
