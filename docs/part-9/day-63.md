# Day 63: Consecration for Service

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 30-32 minutes*

<ProgressTracker dayNumber={63} />

## Chronological Setting

As God's instructions for the tabernacle continue on Mount Sinai, He now reveals the elaborate consecration ceremony that will set apart Aaron and his sons for priestly service. This seven-day ordination process, involving multiple sacrifices, ritual washing, anointing with oil, and application of blood, demonstrates that serving the holy God requires thorough preparation and sanctification. Additionally, God provides instructions for the altar of incense and the sacred anointing oil, establishing the complete system of worship that will govern Israel's approach to God.

## Historical Context

The **consecration ceremony** for the priests involved an intricate seven-day process that included multiple sacrifices—a sin offering to atone for the priests' own sins, a burnt offering demonstrating complete dedication to God, and a unique "ram of ordination" whose blood was applied to the priests' right ear, thumb, and big toe, symbolizing consecration of hearing, action, and walk.

The **anointing oil**, made from specific spices and olive oil, was poured over Aaron's head in such abundance that it ran down his beard and onto his garments (Psalm 133:2). This anointing symbolized the Holy Spirit's empowerment for service and set apart everything it touched as "most holy" to the LORD.

The **altar of incense**, placed in the Holy Place directly in front of the veil separating it from the Most Holy Place, was to have incense burned on it every morning and evening when the lamps were tended. This perpetual sweet fragrance ascending before God's presence symbolized the prayers of God's people rising continually to Him.

## Redemptive Significance

The **elaborate consecration process** reveals that approaching God in ministry requires more than human qualification—it demands divine sanctification. The priests couldn't consecrate themselves; God established the process and provided the means through sacrifice, washing, anointing, and blood application.

The **blood applied to ear, hand, and foot** teaches that ministry to God must be sanctified in every dimension—what we hear (ear), what we do (hand), and where we go (foot). Partial consecration isn't acceptable; service to God requires complete dedication of our entire being.

The **seven-day ordination** emphasizes that preparation for ministry takes time and cannot be rushed. The priests remained at the tabernacle entrance for seven complete days, not leaving even for basic needs. This total separation and focus foreshadows the complete dedication required for effective ministry.

The **altar of incense** represents the intercessory role of the priesthood. While the bronze altar in the courtyard dealt with sin through blood sacrifice, the golden altar inside the Holy Place represented ongoing communion and intercession—demonstrating that relationship with God requires both atonement and continual prayer.

## Connecting the Dots

The writer of Hebrews contrasts the **temporary Aaronic priesthood** with **Christ's eternal priesthood**. Where Aaron and his sons died and needed successors, Christ "continues forever" with an "indestructible life." Where the Levitical priests needed consecration to deal with their own sins before ministering for others, Christ is "holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners."

The **anointing oil** that sanctified Aaron points to Jesus as the "Anointed One" (Messiah/Christ), anointed not with physical oil but with the Holy Spirit without measure. The oil running down Aaron's head and garments symbolizes how Christ's anointing overflows to His body, the church, empowering all believers for ministry.

The **altar of incense** finds its fulfillment in Christ's ongoing intercession at the Father's right hand. Revelation 8:3-4 shows an angel offering incense "with the prayers of all the saints," depicting how Christ presents our prayers before God's throne. Our prayers, offered through Christ our high priest, rise as a sweet fragrance before God.

The **blood on ear, hand, and foot** points to Christ's complete consecration for His priestly work. He perfectly heard the Father's voice, did only what pleased the Father, and walked in complete obedience even to death on the cross—thereby becoming both the perfect priest and the perfect sacrifice.

## Theological Threads

- **Consecration Through Blood**: The extensive use of blood in the ordination ceremony emphasizes that separation unto God requires sacrifice. Sin must be atoned for and life must be given before ministry can begin. This points to the cross as the ultimate consecration, where Christ's blood sanctifies all who believe.

- **Holy Spirit's Anointing**: The anointing oil symbolizes the Holy Spirit's empowerment for ministry. Just as the priests couldn't serve without anointing, believers cannot minister effectively without the Spirit's power. God doesn't call us to serve in our own strength but provides supernatural enablement through His Spirit.

- **Priestly Intercession**: The altar of incense, positioned before the veil, represents the mediatorial role of the priesthood—standing between God and people, offering prayers and intercession. Christ fulfills this perfectly, forever living to intercede for those who draw near to God through Him.

- **Complete Dedication**: The application of blood to ear, hand, and foot, along with the seven-day confinement, demonstrates that ministry requires total consecration. Half-hearted service dishonors God. True ministry flows from complete surrender of all we are and all we have to God's purposes.

## Study Questions

1. **Consecration Process**: The priests underwent an elaborate seven-day consecration before serving. What does this teach about preparation for ministry? How might you be more intentional about preparing yourself spiritually before serving God or others?

2. **Blood Application**: Blood was applied to the priests' right ear, thumb, and big toe. What does this symbolize about consecrated living? How can you practically consecrate your hearing (what you listen to), your actions (what you do), and your walk (where you go) to God?

3. **Anointing for Service**: The anointing oil empowered the priests for ministry, symbolizing the Holy Spirit. How have you experienced the Spirit's empowerment for service? What might it look like to depend more fully on the Spirit's power rather than your own abilities?

4. **Incense and Prayer**: The altar of incense represents continual prayer rising before God. How consistent is your prayer life? What practices help you maintain an attitude of ongoing communion with God throughout your day?

5. **Christ's Eternal Priesthood**: How does understanding Christ as your eternal high priest, who never needs replacement and perfectly intercedes for you, affect your confidence in approaching God? What difference does it make that Christ presents your prayers before the Father?
