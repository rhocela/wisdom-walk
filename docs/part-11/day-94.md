# Day 94: The Inheritance Begins

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 28-30 minutes*

<ProgressTracker dayNumber={94} />

## Chronological Setting

Joshua is now "old and advanced in years" (Joshua 13:1), yet much of the Promised Land remains unconquered. God commands him to divide the land among the tribes even before complete conquest, demonstrating that God's promises are secure enough to distribute in faith. This marks a crucial transition from unified military campaign to tribal settlement, from conquest to inheritance. The detailed allocation of territories shows God's faithfulness to His covenant promises made generations earlier to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—specific land for specific descendants.

## Historical Context

Joshua 13 opens with a sobering reality check: "You are old and advanced in years, and there remains yet very much land to possess" (13:1). Despite the victories chronicled in chapters 10-12, the conquest is incomplete. The Philistine cities along the coast, Phoenician territory to the north, and pockets throughout the land remain in Canaanite hands. Yet God commands Joshua to proceed with dividing the inheritance—a remarkable act of faith.

The chapter recounts the **Transjordan allocation** already given to Reuben, Gad, and half of Manasseh under Moses (Numbers 32). Significantly, the tribe of Levi receives "no inheritance" of land because "the LORD God of Israel is their inheritance" (13:33)—a principle pointing to the greater spiritual inheritance all believers receive in God Himself.

Joshua 14 introduces **Caleb**, the 85-year-old warrior who, with Joshua, had faithfully spied out the land 45 years earlier (Numbers 13-14). While that entire generation died in the wilderness for unbelief, Caleb's faith is rewarded. He requests Hebron—not an easy retirement but the mountain stronghold of the Anakim, the very giants that had terrified the other spies! His testimony reveals the secret of sustained spiritual vitality: "I wholly followed the LORD my God" (14:8).

## Redemptive Significance

The land distribution points to **God's covenant faithfulness**. What He promised to Abraham in Genesis 12:7 ("To your offspring I will give this land") begins to materialize in specific, measurable ways. Each tribe's territory represents God keeping His word across generations.

Yet the "land yet to possess" introduces a sobering theme: **incomplete obedience** results in incomplete blessing. The Israelites' failure to fully drive out the inhabitants will haunt them through the book of Judges and beyond. This foreshadows a recurring biblical pattern—God's people receiving incredible promises but failing to fully appropriate them through faith and obedience.

Caleb stands as a model of **persevering faith**. Despite 45 years of wandering in the wilderness (not his fault!), he doesn't grow bitter or weary. Instead, at 85, he's as passionate for God's purposes as he was at 40. His request for the most difficult territory shows that true faith doesn't seek comfort but opportunities to see God's power displayed.

The **Levitical inheritance**—receiving God rather than land—points forward to the New Testament reality that believers' true inheritance is not earthly territory but God Himself, Christ Himself, and eternal life in His presence.

## Connecting the Dots

Ephesians 1:11-14 reveals that Christians have been given an **inheritance** far surpassing physical land. We've been "predestined according to the purpose of him who works all things according to the counsel of his will" (1:11). The Holy Spirit serves as the "guarantee of our inheritance until we acquire possession of it" (1:14)—like a down payment securing the full future reality.

Colossians 1:12 describes believers as those whom the Father "has qualified... to share in the inheritance of the saints in light." Notice the past tense: "has qualified"—not "will qualify" or "might qualify." Through Christ's finished work, we already possess the title deed to our eternal inheritance, even as we await its full realization.

Just as Israel had to **possess** by faith what God had promised, believers must "take hold" of our inheritance in Christ. We don't earn it—it's given freely. But we must walk in faith, claiming God's promises, just as Caleb claimed Hebron at age 85.

The **incomplete conquest** warns us that we can have genuine faith yet fail to fully appropriate all God has for us. How much of our spiritual inheritance remains "land yet to possess" because of fear, unbelief, compromise, or spiritual laziness?

## Theological Threads

- **God's Covenant Faithfulness**: God remembers His promises across generations. What He swore to Abraham, He fulfills through Joshua. Our God is "not a man, that he should lie" (Numbers 23:19). Every promise He makes, He keeps.

- **Faith and Inheritance**: The land is both **gift** and **task**. God gives it freely, but Israel must take possession by faith. Similarly, our salvation is pure grace, yet we're called to "work out" what God has "worked in" us (Philippians 2:12-13). We possess what we appropriate by faith.

- **Incomplete Obedience, Incomplete Blessing**: The "land yet to possess" becomes a source of future trouble. What Israel fails to conquer eventually conquers them spiritually. Tolerating sin and compromise in our lives—failing to fully "drive out" what God commands—inevitably leads to spiritual defeat.

- **God as Ultimate Inheritance**: The Levites' inheritance is the LORD Himself (13:33). This points to the ultimate reality: knowing God is the supreme treasure. All earthly inheritances fade; God alone satisfies eternally. As the psalmist declares, "The LORD is my chosen portion and my cup" (Psalm 16:5).

- **Persevering Faith**: Caleb's sustained passion after 45 years of waiting shows that genuine faith endures. He doesn't merely survive the wilderness—he emerges stronger, his vision clearer, his faith more robust. True faith grows through trials rather than withering.

## Study Questions

1. **Incomplete Conquest**: Joshua was told to divide the land even though "very much land remains to be possessed." What does this teach about moving forward in faith even when the task seems unfinished? Where in your life is God calling you to act on His promises despite incomplete circumstances?

2. **Caleb's Example**: At 85, Caleb requested the most difficult territory—the mountain stronghold of the giants. What sustained his faith through 45 years of wilderness wandering? How can you cultivate the kind of faith that grows stronger, not weaker, through trials and delays?

3. **Spiritual Inheritance**: The Levites received "the LORD" as their inheritance rather than land. How does this challenge modern materialism and the pursuit of earthly security? What does it mean practically for God to be your inheritance today?

4. **Land Yet to Possess**: What areas of spiritual growth or obedience in your life remain "unconquered territory"? What giants (fears, habits, sins, doubts) do you need to face in God's strength rather than avoid or tolerate?

5. **Guaranteed Inheritance**: Ephesians describes the Holy Spirit as the "guarantee" of our inheritance. How does knowing you already possess the down payment of eternal life affect your daily priorities, anxieties, and sense of security?

6. **Whole-Hearted Following**: Caleb's testimony was "I wholly followed the LORD my God." On a scale of 1-10, how would you rate your current level of whole-hearted devotion to God? What specific step could you take this week to follow Him more completely?
