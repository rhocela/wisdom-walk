# Day 91: Choose This Day Whom You Will Serve

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 30-32 minutes*

## Chronological Setting

After the devastating judgment of Achan and the cleansing of Israel's camp, God speaks to Joshua with words of reassurance: "Do not fear and do not be dismayed." The sin has been dealt with, the devoted things removed, and God's presence restored to His people. Now Israel can resume the conquest. God commands Joshua to take all the fighting men and attack Ai again—but this time with divine strategy and divine blessing. Using a military ambush, Israel defeats Ai decisively. Following this victory, Joshua leads the nation north to Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim, where—in obedience to Moses' command (Deuteronomy 27)—he builds an altar, writes the law on stones, and leads the people in renewing their covenant with the LORD. Blessings and curses are read aloud, with half the tribes standing on Mount Gerizim for the blessings and half on Mount Ebal for the curses. This covenant renewal ceremony demonstrates that victory in the Promised Land depends not on military might but on faithfulness to God's covenant.

## Historical Context

**God's Reassurance and Strategy (Joshua 8:1-2)**: After Achan's sin brought defeat, God now says, "Do not fear... Take all the fighting men with you, and arise, go up to Ai. See, I have given into your hand the king of Ai, and his people, his city, and his land." The command to take "all the fighting men" contrasts with the previous overconfident strategy of sending only 2,000-3,000 men. God also permits Israel to take the spoil and livestock of Ai as plunder—different from Jericho, which was under the ban. God tells Joshua to set an ambush behind the city.

**The Battle Strategy (Joshua 8:3-29)**: Joshua implements a sophisticated military strategy. He sends 30,000 warriors by night to lie in ambush west of Ai, between Ai and Bethel. Joshua himself takes a smaller force and positions them north of the city. When the king of Ai sees Israel's main force, he and his army rush out to attack, thinking they can defeat Israel as before. But Joshua's force feigns retreat, drawing Ai's army away from the city—"Not a man was left in Ai or Bethel who did not go out after Israel." At God's signal, Joshua stretches out his javelin toward the city, and the ambush rises, enters the undefended city, and sets it on fire. When the men of Ai look back and see their city burning, they are trapped between two Israelite forces with nowhere to flee. Israel destroys Ai completely—12,000 men and women—and burns the city. Joshua hangs the king of Ai on a tree until evening, then takes down the body and throws it at the city gate, covering it with stones as a memorial.

**Covenant Renewal at Mount Ebal (Joshua 8:30-35)**: After the victory at Ai, Joshua leads the entire nation—fighting men, women, children, and sojourners—north to the valley between Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim, near Shechem. This location has deep patriarchal significance: Abraham built an altar here (Genesis 12:6-7), and Jacob buried foreign gods here under the oak (Genesis 35:4). Following Moses' explicit command in Deuteronomy 27, Joshua builds an altar of uncut stones on Mount Ebal and offers burnt offerings and peace offerings to the LORD. He writes a copy of the law of Moses on plastered stones. Then, with the ark of the covenant positioned in the valley and the Levitical priests surrounding it, half the tribes stand on Mount Gerizim (for the blessing) and half on Mount Ebal (for the curse). Joshua reads aloud every word of the law—all the blessings and curses—to the assembled congregation. Not one word of Moses' law is left unread.

## Redemptive Significance

**Restoration After Repentance**: The contrast between Joshua 7 and Joshua 8 is striking. Sin brought defeat; repentance and obedience bring victory. God's promise "I have given into your hand" signals that His presence and blessing are restored. This pattern recurs throughout Scripture: when God's people confess and forsake sin, God restores and blesses. The New Testament teaches the same principle—**confession brings cleansing** (1 John 1:9), and cleansing restores fellowship with God and effectiveness in His service.

**Obedience to God's Word**: The covenant renewal ceremony at Mount Ebal demonstrates Israel's commitment to live under God's word. Every blessing and curse is read aloud, reminding the nation that their success in the land depends entirely on covenant faithfulness. This public reading of the law emphasizes that **God's word must govern God's people**. In the same way, the church today lives under the authority of Scripture, and our spiritual health depends on hearing, believing, and obeying God's revealed word.

**Victory Through Divine Strategy**: The ambush at Ai was sophisticated military strategy, yet it came by divine instruction. God is not opposed to human effort and wisdom—He designed us to think, plan, and act strategically. But **human strategy succeeds only when submitted to divine authority**. When we seek God's wisdom and follow His leading, He blesses our efforts. When we rely on our own understanding without God, even the best plans fail.

**The Tree and the Stones**: Joshua hangs the king of Ai on a tree until evening, then buries him under stones—fulfilling the law of Deuteronomy 21:22-23, which says, "A hanged man is cursed by God; you shall not defile the land." This detail points forward to Christ, who was "hanged on a tree" to bear the curse for us (Galatians 3:13). The king of Ai died under God's curse for his sin; Jesus died under God's curse for our sin. The heap of stones over Ai's king is a memorial to judgment; the empty tomb is a memorial to victory over the curse.

## Connecting the Dots

**Dead to Sin, Alive to God**: Paul's teaching in Romans 6 connects powerfully to Achan's judgment and Israel's restoration. Just as Achan's sin had to be dealt with decisively before Israel could move forward, so believers must reckon themselves "dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus" (Romans 6:11). **Sin has been judged at the cross**, and we are no longer enslaved to it. When we sin, we confess it, receive cleansing, and walk in newness of life—just as Israel, after dealing with Achan's sin, experienced renewed victory.

**Walking in the Light**: First John 1:5-10 presents the principle demonstrated in Joshua 7-8. When we walk in darkness—hiding sin like Achan—we break fellowship with God and forfeit His blessing. But when we "walk in the light," confessing our sins openly before God, "He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." The path from defeat to victory runs through confession and cleansing.

**The Law Written on Stones and Hearts**: Joshua wrote the law on plastered stones at Mount Ebal, creating a permanent visible reminder of God's covenant. But this external law, while good and holy, could not save Israel from breaking it. The prophets foretold a new covenant when God would write His law not on stones but on human hearts (Jeremiah 31:33; Ezekiel 36:26-27). Through Christ and the Holy Spirit, God has done this. **The law we could never keep is now written within us** by the Spirit, enabling us to walk in obedience not from external compulsion but from internal transformation.

**The Greater Joshua**: Joshua's name in Hebrew (*Yeshua*) is the same as Jesus' name. Joshua led Israel into the earthly Promised Land through military conquest; Jesus leads His people into the heavenly promised rest through His redemptive conquest over sin and death. Just as Joshua commanded Israel to be strong and courageous, obeying all the law, Jesus sends His disciples to make disciples of all nations, "teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you" (Matthew 28:20). The greater Joshua not only commands but empowers obedience through His Spirit.

## Theological Threads

- **Divine Sovereignty and Human Responsibility**: God promised to give Ai into Israel's hand, yet He commanded Joshua to use strategic military tactics. This illustrates the biblical balance: God is sovereign and accomplishes His purposes, but He works through human agency and effort. We are called to plan, work, and fight, trusting that God will bless our obedience.

- **Covenant Blessing and Curse**: The ceremony at Mount Ebal emphasizes the two-fold nature of God's covenant: obedience brings blessing, disobedience brings curse. This prepares us for the gospel, which reveals that Christ became a curse for us (Galatians 3:13) so that we might receive the blessing of Abraham—justification by faith and the gift of the Spirit.

- **The Word as Central**: Joshua read "all the words of the law, the blessing and the curse" without exception. God's people are to be shaped by God's word entirely, not selectively. This anticipates the New Testament emphasis on Scripture as God-breathed and profitable for teaching, reproof, correction, and training in righteousness (2 Timothy 3:16-17).

- **Memorials of God's Works**: The heap of stones at Ai, like the stones at the Jordan crossing and the altar at Ebal, serves as a memorial. God commanded Israel to create physical reminders of His works so future generations would ask, "What do these stones mean?" and be taught the LORD's faithfulness. Similarly, the Lord's Supper serves as a memorial of Christ's death "until he comes" (1 Corinthians 11:26).

## Study Questions

1. **From Defeat to Victory**: Compare Joshua 7 (defeat at Ai) with Joshua 8 (victory at Ai). What changed between these two battles? How does dealing decisively with sin in your life open the door to spiritual victory?

2. **God's Strategy**: God gave Joshua specific military instructions for conquering Ai. How do you seek God's wisdom and direction in the challenges you face? What's the difference between godly planning and self-reliant scheming?

3. **Covenant Renewal**: Why did Joshua lead Israel in covenant renewal immediately after the victory at Ai? What does this teach about the importance of continually reaffirming our commitment to God's word? How can you regularly "renew your covenant" with God?

4. **Blessings and Curses**: The reading of blessings and curses at Mount Ebal reminded Israel of the stakes—obedience leads to life, disobedience to death. How does understanding the seriousness of God's commands affect your obedience? How does the gospel free you from the curse while maintaining the call to holiness?

5. **The Hanged King**: Joshua 8:29 describes the king of Ai hanging on a tree under God's curse. How does this point forward to Christ, who hung on a tree to bear our curse (Galatians 3:13)? What does this reveal about the costliness of your redemption?

6. **Walking in the Light**: First John 1:7 says, "If we walk in the light, as he is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus his Son cleanses us from all sin." How does Achan's hidden sin versus Israel's restored obedience illustrate the importance of walking in the light? What sin might you be hiding that needs to be brought into God's light?
