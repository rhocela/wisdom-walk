# Day 91: Choose This Day Whom You Will Serve

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 28-31 minutes*

<ProgressTracker dayNumber={91} />

## Chronological Setting

We arrive at one of Scripture's most pivotal moments—Joshua's farewell addresses to Israel. After decades of conquest and settlement, the aged leader gathers the nation for his final charge. At Shechem, the very place where Abraham first received God's promise (Genesis 12:6-7), Joshua calls Israel to renew their covenant commitment. This occurs around 1390 BC, approximately 30 years after crossing the Jordan. Joshua is now over 110 years old, and his concern is not military strategy but spiritual fidelity. Will the next generation continue to serve the Lord?

## Historical Context

Joshua's farewell addresses follow a pattern common in the ancient Near East, where aging leaders would gather their people for final instructions and covenant renewal. The choice of **Shechem** as the location is profoundly significant. This was where:
- Abraham first built an altar when he entered Canaan (Genesis 12:6-7)
- Jacob buried foreign gods under the oak tree (Genesis 35:2-4)
- The twelve tribes would later divide into two kingdoms (1 Kings 12)

Joshua structures his speech around Israel's redemptive history, reminding them of God's faithfulness from Abraham through the Exodus to the present conquest. His famous declaration—**"As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD"**—isn't merely personal testimony but a bold challenge to make the same commitment.

The **warnings** Joshua gives are not theoretical. He knows that the remaining Canaanite populations pose a spiritual threat more dangerous than any military danger. Intermarriage and idolatry will be Israel's downfall—a prophecy that the book of Judges will soon confirm.

## Redemptive Significance

Joshua's challenge—**"Choose this day whom you will serve"**—reverberates through all of Scripture as God's call to wholehearted devotion. This isn't a one-time decision but a daily, moment-by-moment choice between serving God or serving idols (whether literal gods or the idols of our hearts).

Joshua presents the choice starkly: you cannot serve the LORD and other gods. This exclusivity isn't divine insecurity but theological reality—**Yahweh alone is God**, and to divide allegiance is to deny His nature. This theme continues through:
- **Elijah** at Mount Carmel: "How long will you go limping between two opinions?" (1 Kings 18:21)
- **Jesus** in the Sermon on the Mount: "No one can serve two masters" (Matthew 6:24)
- **Christ** to the church at Laodicea: "I wish that you were either cold or hot" (Revelation 3:15)

The covenant renewal at Shechem foreshadows the New Covenant in Christ's blood. Just as Israel stood before God to pledge obedience, we respond to the gospel with faith and commitment. And just as Joshua warned of consequences for unfaithfulness, the New Testament warns that profession without transformation is empty (James 2:14-26).

## Connecting the Dots

Joshua's farewell connects to the **entire biblical storyline**:

**Backward to Moses**: Joshua echoes Moses' final charge in Deuteronomy 30:15-20—"I have set before you life and death... choose life." Both leaders call Israel to exclusive covenant loyalty.

**Forward to the Prophets**: Joshua's warnings about apostasy prove prophetic. The cycle of sin, judgment, and deliverance in Judges begins almost immediately. Later prophets like Hosea and Jeremiah will plead with Israel to return to the covenant faithfulness Joshua demanded.

**To Christ**: Jesus is the ultimate Joshua (both names mean "Yahweh saves"), who perfectly fulfills the covenant and enables us to live in faithful obedience. What Israel could not do—serve the LORD wholeheartedly—Christ does for us and in us through the Holy Spirit.

**To Our Response**: Like Israel at Shechem, we must answer the gospel call with decisive commitment. "Taste and see that the LORD is good" (Psalm 34:8) is an invitation, but it requires a response. Christ's Lordship demands our wholehearted allegiance.

## Theological Threads

**1. Covenant Faithfulness**

Joshua rehearses Israel's history to show God's unbroken faithfulness—from calling Abraham out of paganism, through the Exodus, to the conquest of Canaan. Every promise God made, He kept (Joshua 23:14). This divine faithfulness is the foundation for Israel's responsive faithfulness.

The covenant is not a contract between equals but a **gracious relationship** initiated by God. Israel's obedience flows from gratitude, not merit. Similarly, our obedience as Christians is a grateful response to grace, not an attempt to earn salvation (Ephesians 2:8-10).

**2. The Danger of Compromise**

Joshua warns that tolerating idolatry will lead to embracing it. **Spiritual compromise is progressive**—what begins as tolerance ends in captivity. The Israelites will learn this painful lesson in Judges, as what they fail to drive out eventually enslaves them.

This principle applies to us. The "little sins" we tolerate, the worldly values we accommodate, the half-hearted obedience we excuse—these become snares that entangle us. Jesus calls us to radical discipleship, not comfortable compromise (Luke 14:25-33).

**3. Generational Responsibility**

Joshua's concern is not just the current generation but those who will come after. Will the next generation know the LORD? This question haunts the book of Judges: "And all that generation also were gathered to their fathers. And there arose another generation after them who did not know the LORD" (Judges 2:10).

Parents and spiritual leaders bear responsibility to pass on faith. This happens not merely through verbal instruction but through visible commitment—"As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD." Children need to see faith lived out authentically.

**4. Public Witness**

Joshua's declaration is **public and unambiguous**. He doesn't hedge his commitment with qualifications or make it contingent on circumstances. His household will serve the LORD—period.

This public witness challenges the people to similar boldness. In our age of religious pluralism and tolerance, we need Joshua's courage to declare our allegiance to Christ clearly and unapologetically. Our witness should provoke a decision in others: "Will you also serve Him?"

**5. The Stone of Witness**

Joshua sets up a stone as a witness to Israel's covenant commitment (Joshua 24:27). This memorial serves as a constant reminder and future testimony. When later generations ask, "What does this stone mean?" they will hear the story of covenant commitment.

Scripture repeatedly uses physical memorials to anchor spiritual truth—the Passover, the Lord's Supper, baptism. We need tangible reminders of our commitments because memory fades and hearts drift. What markers have you established to remember God's faithfulness and your commitments?

## Study Questions

1. **Exclusive Allegiance**: Joshua demanded Israel choose between the LORD and other gods. What "gods" compete for your allegiance today? How does Jesus' statement "No one can serve two masters" challenge your divided loyalties?

2. **Covenant Remembrance**: Joshua rehearsed Israel's history to remind them of God's faithfulness (Joshua 24:2-13). How does remembering God's past faithfulness in your life strengthen your present faith? What specific acts of God's faithfulness can you recount?

3. **Public Commitment**: "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" was a bold public declaration. How visible is your commitment to Christ? In what areas might God be calling you to more courageous witness?

4. **Generational Faithfulness**: Joshua's concern was that the next generation would abandon the LORD. If you have children or influence over younger believers, what are you doing to pass on authentic faith? What did you observe in older believers that shaped your own faith?

5. **The Challenge of "Choose"**: Why does God require us to choose rather than remaining neutral? What does the lukewarm church of Laodicea (Revelation 3:15-16) teach us about the danger of half-hearted commitment?

6. **Covenant Warnings**: Joshua warns of severe consequences for unfaithfulness (Joshua 23:12-13, 15-16). How do you balance God's grace with His holiness? Does knowing the cost of disobedience motivate your obedience, or does it create fear?
