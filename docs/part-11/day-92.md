# Day 86: Deceived But Covenant-Bound

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 27-29 minutes*

<ProgressTracker dayNumber={86} />

## Chronological Setting

News of Israel's victories at Jericho and Ai has spread throughout Canaan, striking fear into the hearts of the nations. The kings west of the Jordan—in the hill country, in the lowland, and along the Mediterranean coast—form a coalition to fight against Israel. But the inhabitants of Gibeon, one of the great cities of Canaan, choose a different strategy: deception. Knowing they cannot defeat Israel militarily and fearing destruction, the Gibeonites disguise themselves as travelers from a distant country and trick Joshua into making a peace treaty with them. By the time Israel discovers the deception—that Gibeon is actually nearby—they have already sworn an oath before the LORD and cannot break it. This episode reveals both human failure (Joshua did not inquire of the LORD before making the treaty) and divine sovereignty (God uses even human mistakes to accomplish His purposes). The Gibeonites become servants of Israel, and later, when five Amorite kings attack Gibeon for making peace with Israel, God commands Joshua to defend his treaty partners—leading to one of the most dramatic miracles in Scripture: the day the sun stood still.

## Historical Context

**The Gibeonite Deception (Joshua 9:1-15)**: When the inhabitants of Gibeon—a major city whose men were mighty warriors—hear of Israel's conquests, they devise a cunning plan. They dress in worn-out clothes and sandals, load their donkeys with old wineskins and moldy bread, and approach Joshua at Gilgal claiming to be ambassadors from "a very distant country." They appeal to Israel's growing reputation: "We have heard a report of him, and all that he did in Egypt, and all that he did to the two kings of the Amorites." The Gibeonites cleverly mention only events from forty years earlier, not the recent victories at Jericho and Ai, to maintain their story of coming from far away. They request a covenant of peace.

The men of Israel are suspicious, asking, "Perhaps you live among us; then how can we make a covenant with you?" But the Gibeonites persist with their deception, showing the worn provisions as "proof" of their long journey. **Critically, verse 14 says: "So the men took some of their provisions, but did not ask counsel from the LORD."** Joshua makes a peace treaty with them and swears an oath before the LORD to let them live.

**The Discovery (Joshua 9:16-27)**: Three days later, Israel discovers that Gibeon and three other cities are actually nearby neighbors within the land God promised to Israel. The congregation grumbles against their leaders, but Joshua and the elders explain that they cannot break the oath sworn in the LORD's name—to do so would bring the LORD's wrath upon Israel. Instead, they make the Gibeonites "cutters of wood and drawers of water for the congregation and for the altar of the LORD"—perpetual servants, but protected under the covenant. When Joshua confronts them about their deception, the Gibeonites explain their motive: "Because it was told to your servants for a certainty that the LORD your God had commanded his servant Moses to give you all the land and to destroy all the inhabitants of the land from before you—so we feared greatly for our lives because of you."

**The Five Kings Attack Gibeon (Joshua 10:1-5)**: Adoni-zedek, king of Jerusalem, hears that Gibeon has made peace with Israel and becomes alarmed. Gibeon was a royal city, larger than Ai, with mighty warriors—its defection to Israel is a major strategic loss. He forms a coalition with four other Amorite kings (Hebron, Jarmuth, Lachish, Eglon) and attacks Gibeon for betraying the Canaanite cause.

**The All-Night March and Divine Intervention (Joshua 10:6-15)**: The Gibeonites send urgent word to Joshua at Gilgal: "Do not relax your hand from your servants. Come up to us quickly and save us and help us." The LORD tells Joshua, "Do not fear them, for I have given them into your hand. Not a man of them shall stand before you." Joshua marches all night—about 20 miles uphill from Gilgal to Gibeon—and attacks at dawn, catching the enemy by surprise. The LORD throws the coalition into panic, and as they flee down the ascent of Beth-horon, **the LORD throws large hailstones from heaven**, killing more with hailstones than Israel kills with the sword.

Then comes one of Scripture's most dramatic moments. Joshua prays publicly: "Sun, stand still at Gibeon, and moon, in the Valley of Aijalon." And **the sun and moon stop moving** until Israel defeats their enemies. The text declares: "There has been no day like it before or since, when the LORD obeyed the voice of a man, for the LORD fought for Israel."

## Redemptive Significance

**Human Failure, Divine Sovereignty**: Joshua's failure to inquire of the LORD before making the treaty with Gibeon is a clear mistake. Yet God uses this failure redemptively. The Gibeonites become servants of the altar—a picture of Gentiles being brought into God's covenant people, serving in His house. Later, when Saul violates this covenant and tries to exterminate the Gibeonites (2 Samuel 21), God judges Israel with famine until the covenant is honored. **God's purposes are not derailed by human mistakes**; He weaves even our failures into His sovereign plan.

**The Oath's Sanctity**: Israel keeps their oath to the Gibeonites despite being deceived because it was sworn in the LORD's name. This demonstrates the absolute sanctity of vows made before God. Jesus later teaches that we should simply let our "yes" be yes and our "no" be no (Matthew 5:33-37), avoiding oath-making altogether because of its binding nature. But when oaths are made in God's name, **they must be kept**, for God's honor is at stake.

**Fear and Faith**: The Gibeonites' deception arose from genuine fear of Israel's God. They heard of His mighty works and believed the reports enough to seek peace rather than war. Their faith, though mixed with deception, was more perceptive than the other Canaanite nations who chose to fight. This parallels Rahab, whose faith—also expressed through deception (hiding the spies)—led to her salvation. Both stories show that **God honors faith even when it's imperfect**, and He welcomes those who turn to Him, even from among Israel's enemies.

**The LORD Fights for Israel**: The miracle of the sun standing still demonstrates that the conquest of Canaan is ultimately the LORD's work, not Israel's. When the battle requires more daylight, God extends the day. When hailstones can kill more effectively than swords, God sends hailstones. **The battle is the LORD's**, and He will accomplish His purposes by whatever means necessary. This anticipates the greater truth: salvation is the LORD's work from beginning to end, and He will bring His people safely into their inheritance.

## Connecting the Dots

**Discernment and Deception**: Paul warns that "Satan disguises himself as an angel of light" (2 Corinthians 11:14). The Gibeonites appeared harmless—worn travelers seeking peace—but their appearance was deceptive. Similarly, false teachers and false doctrines often come with appealing exteriors. **The antidote is to inquire of the LORD**—to test everything by Scripture, to seek God's wisdom, to not rely on appearances. Joshua's failure to consult God led to a binding commitment to potential enemies. Believers must seek divine wisdom before making major decisions.

**Ask for Wisdom**: James 1:5 promises, "If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given him." Joshua's mistake was not moral failure but a failure to seek God's wisdom. The text pointedly notes: they "did not ask counsel from the LORD" (Joshua 9:14). **God's wisdom is available, but we must ask for it**. When facing complex decisions, spiritual discernment, or potential deception, we must turn to God in prayer and search His word for guidance.

**The Greater Conquest**: Joshua's conquest of Canaan through miraculous divine intervention points to Christ's conquest of sin, death, and Satan. Just as God made the sun stand still to ensure Israel's victory, so God raised Christ from the dead to ensure our victory. **The resurrection is God's greatest intervention in history**, demonstrating that nothing—not death itself—can prevent God from delivering His people into their promised inheritance.

**Gentile Inclusion**: The Gibeonites, though Canaanites under God's judgment, are incorporated into Israel through covenant. They become "cutters of wood and drawers of water" for the house of God—servants, yes, but protected and included. This foreshadows the gospel reality that Gentiles who were "alienated from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers to the covenants of promise" (Ephesians 2:12) are brought near through Christ's blood and made fellow heirs with Israel. **God's covenant people include both Jew and Gentile**, united in faith.

## Theological Threads

- **God's Sovereignty Over Human Mistakes**: Joshua made a significant error by not consulting the LORD, yet God's plan continued. This doesn't excuse our failures, but it assures us that **God's purposes are not derailed by our mistakes**. He is able to work all things—even our errors—together for good (Romans 8:28).

- **The Necessity of Seeking God's Wisdom**: Scripture repeatedly emphasizes that human wisdom is insufficient. We need divine wisdom for spiritual discernment, decision-making, and detecting deception. Proverbs 3:5-6 says, "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make straight your paths."

- **The Binding Nature of Oaths**: When Israel swore an oath in the LORD's name, they were bound by it even though they were deceived. This shows the seriousness of invoking God's name and making commitments. God's reputation is tied to our word when we swear by His name, which is why Jesus teaches us to avoid oaths and simply speak truthfully (Matthew 5:33-37).

- **God's Miraculous Power**: The sun standing still is one of Scripture's most dramatic miracles, demonstrating God's absolute control over creation. He can stop celestial bodies, send hailstones, and alter the laws of nature to accomplish His purposes. **Nothing is too hard for the LORD** (Genesis 18:14; Jeremiah 32:17).

## Study Questions

1. **Failure to Inquire**: Joshua 9:14 says Israel "did not ask counsel from the LORD." When have you made decisions based on appearances or circumstances without seeking God's wisdom? What was the result? How can you build a practice of consulting God before major decisions?

2. **Honoring Commitments**: Israel kept their oath to the Gibeonites despite being deceived. What does this teach about integrity and keeping our word, even when it's costly? How does Jesus' teaching in Matthew 5:33-37 ("let your yes be yes") relate to this account?

3. **Fear That Leads to Faith**: The Gibeonites feared Israel's God and sought peace, while other nations feared and chose war. What's the difference between fear that leads to faith and fear that leads to opposition? How can fear of God be a healthy starting point for relationship with Him?

4. **Detecting Deception**: The Gibeonites' worn clothes and moldy bread fooled Israel. What are some ways Satan disguises lies to make them appear attractive or harmless? How can you develop spiritual discernment to recognize deception?

5. **The Sun Stands Still**: God performed an unprecedented miracle—making the sun stand still—to ensure Israel's victory. What does this teach about God's commitment to His people and His purposes? How does this encourage you when facing seemingly impossible obstacles?

6. **Gentiles in God's Plan**: The Gibeonites became servants in God's house, foreshadowing Gentile inclusion in the covenant. How does this story help you understand God's heart for all nations? How should this shape your view of missions and evangelism?
