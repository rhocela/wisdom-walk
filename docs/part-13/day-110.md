# Day 110: Faith Finds Favor

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 29-31 minutes*

<ProgressTracker dayNumber={110} />

## Chronological Setting
Continuing Ruth's story during the barley harvest season (c. 1100 BC), we witness how God's providence works through everyday circumstances to provide for His people. Ruth's initiative to glean in the fields represents both practical necessity and remarkable courage for a foreign widow. Her "chance" encounter with Boaz reveals how God orchestrates seemingly random events to accomplish His redemptive purposes. This chapter shows God's faithfulness to provide for the poor and vulnerable through the kindness of covenant-keeping people.

## Historical Context
The famine that drives Elimelech's family to Moab reflects the agricultural difficulties during the judges period when "everyone did what was right in his own eyes." Moab, while geographically close to Bethlehem, represented spiritual danger as a pagan nation with a history of seducing Israel into idolatry. The deaths of Elimelech and his sons leave three vulnerable widows in a patriarchal society where women depended on male relatives for survival and protection. Ruth's decision to accompany Naomi demonstrates remarkable covenant loyalty (hesed)—the same faithful love God shows His people despite their unfaithfulness.

## Redemptive Significance
Ruth's story reveals God's heart for the nations and His plan to include Gentiles in His covenant family. Her inclusion in Christ's genealogy demonstrates that the Messiah came not just for Jews but for all who would demonstrate faith and covenant loyalty like Ruth. Where Israel often abandoned God for foreign gods, this foreign woman abandons her gods to follow the true God of Israel. Ruth's faithful love for Naomi reflects the hesed that God shows His people, and her story points forward to the greater love story of Christ's faithful love for His bride, the church.

## Connecting the Dots
Peter's teaching about living hope through suffering directly applies to Ruth and Naomi's experience of loss and displacement, showing how God can work even tragic circumstances for His glory. Paul's declaration that God works all things for good is beautifully illustrated in Ruth's story, where famine, death, and displacement become the means by which a Moabite woman enters the messianic line. The passage from Ephesians about foreigners being brought near perfectly describes Ruth's journey from pagan outsider to covenant insider, foreshadowing how all believers are brought into God's family through Christ.

## Theological Threads
- **Covenant Loyalty (Hesed)**: True faithfulness is demonstrated through commitment that persists despite changed circumstances and personal cost
- **Divine Providence**: God's sovereign hand guides events even when His presence seems hidden, working through ordinary human choices to accomplish extraordinary purposes  
- **Inclusive Grace**: God's covenant love extends beyond ethnic Israel to include all who demonstrate faith and loyalty, anticipating the gospel's reach to all nations
- **Redemptive Reversal**: What appears to be tragic loss can become the pathway to greater blessing when surrendered to God's purposes

## Study Questions
1. **Covenant Loyalty**: What does Ruth's decision to stay with Naomi despite having every reason to return home teach us about true commitment and love?

2. **God's Hidden Hand**: How do you see God's providence working even in the apparent tragedies of this chapter, and how does this encourage you in difficult times?

3. **Inclusive Gospel**: How does Ruth's story challenge any tendency we might have to see Christianity as exclusive to certain ethnic or cultural groups?

4. **Faith in Darkness**: Both Ruth and Naomi are living in what seems like hopeless circumstances—how does their story encourage faithfulness even when we can't see God's plan?

5. **Costly Commitment**: Ruth gives up her homeland, culture, and potential remarriage to care for Naomi—what does this teach us about the nature of true love and loyalty?
