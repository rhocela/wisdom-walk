# Day 113: Redemption Accomplished

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 31-33 minutes*

<ProgressTracker dayNumber={113} />

## Chronological Setting
We reach the triumphant conclusion of Ruth's story (c. 1100 BC), which ends with one of the most significant genealogical notes in the Old Testament—Ruth becomes the great-grandmother of King David. This climactic chapter demonstrates how God's sovereign providence works through human faithfulness and legal processes to accomplish His redemptive purposes. The story concludes during the dark period of the judges with a bright hope pointing toward Israel's greatest king and, ultimately, toward the coming Messiah.

## Historical Context
The legal proceedings at the city gate follow established procedures for property redemption and marriage obligations. The nearer kinsman's refusal to redeem demonstrates both the costliness and the voluntary nature of redemption—he could not afford to risk his own inheritance. Boaz's willingness to pay the price reveals his generous love and commitment. The removal of the sandal symbolized the transfer of rights and became a witness to the transaction. The women's blessing of Ruth and their comparison to Rachel and Leah shows her full acceptance into the covenant community. The genealogy ending with David reveals God's providential orchestration of this love story to advance His messianic purposes.

## Redemptive Significance
Boaz's role as kinsman-redeemer provides one of the clearest Old Testament pictures of Christ's redemptive work. Just as Boaz was willing and able to pay the price for Ruth's redemption, Christ willingly paid the ultimate price for our salvation. The marriage that follows redemption beautifully illustrates the union between Christ and His redeemed people. Ruth's transformation from foreign widow to honored mother in the messianic line demonstrates the complete reversal that redemption brings. The genealogy connecting Ruth to David and ultimately to Christ shows how God weaves individual stories of faithfulness into His grand plan of salvation.

## Connecting the Dots
The Levitical laws about kinsman-redemption find their ultimate fulfillment in Christ, who became our kinsman through the incarnation and redeemed us through His sacrifice. Paul's teaching about God sending His Son to redeem us directly connects to Boaz's initiative in redeeming Ruth. The cost of redemption described in Titus and 1 Peter—Christ's own life and blood—shows the infinitely greater price our Redeemer paid compared to Boaz's material cost. The progression from redemption to adoption in Galatians mirrors Ruth's journey from outsider to beloved family member, culminating in her inclusion in the royal lineage.

## Theological Threads
- **Completed Redemption**: True redemption involves both paying the price and claiming the redeemed as one's own, leading to transformation and new identity
- **Providential Purpose**: God orchestrates individual acts of faithfulness and love to accomplish His larger redemptive plan across generations
- **Costly Grace**: Redemption requires a willing redeemer who can and will pay whatever price is necessary, regardless of personal cost
- **Inclusive Kingdom**: God's kingdom welcomes all who demonstrate faith and loyalty, regardless of ethnic or social background, into the highest privileges of His family

## Study Questions
1. **Willing Redeemer**: What does Boaz's eagerness to redeem Ruth teach us about Christ's attitude toward redeeming us, and how should this affect our relationship with Him?

2. **Cost of Redemption**: How does the contrast between the nearer kinsman's refusal and Boaz's willingness help us understand the nature of Christ's sacrifice for us?

3. **Complete Transformation**: What aspects of Ruth's transformation from foreign widow to honored mother reflect the complete change that occurs when Christ redeems us?

4. **Generational Impact**: How does the genealogy ending with David show us that our faithfulness can have impacts far beyond what we can see or imagine?

5. **Divine Providence**: Looking back over Ruth's entire story, how do you see God's hand working through ordinary choices to accomplish extraordinary purposes, and how does this encourage your own faith?
