# Day 112: A Bold Request for Redemption

⏱️ *Estimated reading time: 25-28 minutes*

## Chronological Setting
We continue in Ruth's story during the harvest season (c. 1100 BC), as the narrative moves toward its climactic moment. After weeks of Ruth faithfully gleaning in Boaz's fields, Naomi devises a plan that will test both Ruth's courage and Boaz's character. This chapter represents a pivotal moment where human initiative intersects with divine providence, as Ruth takes the bold step of approaching her potential kinsman-redeemer. The customs depicted here reflect ancient Near Eastern marriage and redemption practices that point toward profound spiritual truths.

## Historical Context
Ruth's nighttime approach to Boaz at the threshing floor follows ancient customs for invoking kinsman-redeemer rights. The phrase "spread your wings over your servant" (3:9) echoes Boaz's earlier blessing that Ruth had come under God's wings for refuge (2:12), now requesting that Boaz become the human instrument of that divine protection. The threshing floor was a place where business was conducted and legal matters settled. Ruth's actions, while bold, were proper within the cultural context—she was formally requesting marriage and redemption according to established customs. Boaz's integrity is revealed in his protection of Ruth's reputation and his honest acknowledgment of a closer kinsman.

## Redemptive Significance
Ruth's approach to Boaz beautifully foreshadows the soul's approach to Christ for redemption. Just as Ruth came humbly seeking covering and protection from her kinsman-redeemer, we must come to Christ acknowledging our need for His redemptive covering. Boaz's immediate acceptance of responsibility and his concern for Ruth's welfare reflect Christ's eager willingness to redeem those who seek Him. The marriage imagery that permeates this chapter points to the ultimate union between Christ and His bride, the church, demonstrating that human marriage finds its deepest meaning as a picture of divine love.

## Connecting the Dots
Isaiah's prophecy about God as both Husband and Redeemer connects directly to Ruth's story, showing how human redemption pictures divine marriage love. Paul's teaching about Christ's sacrificial love for the church illuminates the spiritual reality behind Boaz's willingness to redeem Ruth at personal cost. The progression from betrothal (2 Corinthians) to wedding feast (Revelation) shows the complete arc of salvation that Ruth's story foreshadows. Ruth's boldness in approaching Boaz reflects the confidence we can have in approaching Christ for salvation, knowing He desires to redeem us.

## Theological Threads
- **Bold Faith**: True faith sometimes requires bold action, trusting God's character and promises when taking steps that require courage
- **Kinsman-Redeemer**: The kinsman-redeemer concept reveals God's desire to enter our condition to rescue us, pointing to the incarnation and atonement
- **Divine-Human Marriage**: The marriage relationship serves as the primary biblical metaphor for God's covenant love relationship with His people
- **Providential Timing**: God orchestrates circumstances and relationships to accomplish His redemptive purposes through willing human participants

## Study Questions
1. **Courage and Faith**: What does Ruth's bold approach to Boaz teach us about taking faith-filled action when seeking God's provision and redemption?

2. **Kinsman-Redeemer**: How does Boaz's role as kinsman-redeemer help us understand what Christ has done for us as our ultimate Redeemer?

3. **Marriage Imagery**: How does the relationship developing between Ruth and Boaz deepen your understanding of Christ's love for the church?

4. **Divine Providence**: How do you see God's hand working through human choices and cultural customs to accomplish His redemptive plan in this chapter?

5. **Personal Application**: In what areas of your life might God be calling you to approach Him with the same bold faith that Ruth showed in approaching Boaz?
